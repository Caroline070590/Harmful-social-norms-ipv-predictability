# Exploratory country-context prediction comparison

Saved 19 October 2026 before fitting this extension. This is a post-primary, exploratory analysis, not preregistration or independent replication. The earlier GFS null prediction comparison, Colombian symptom associations, source response frequencies and country linkage availability have already been inspected. Do not tune this comparison after seeing its results.

Question: do historical female country attitude profiles improve out-of-country prediction of the UN Women Phase II symptom items beyond available individual characteristics? This concerns retrospective cross-country prediction, not prospective prediction after infection or an estimate of a causal effect.

Use the prepared public UN Women release and audited country crosswalk. Primary comparison: eight countries with historical national female any-reason acceptance. Secondary complete-profile comparison: six countries with that feature plus complete five-item female education and rural contrasts. Use identical complete respondents for every model and both outcomes within each comparison. Do not compare errors across the two different country sets as if they were matched.

Outcomes, separately: Phase II worry item (0–3), and sum of Phase II anhedonia and depressed-mood items (0–6). Their since-COVID recall differs from standard two-week PHQ/GAD scales. Do not call them GAD-2, PHQ-2, diagnoses, pre/post changes or COVID-caused disease. Do not pool Phase I stress items. No threshold analysis, correlations selected for significance or hypothesis-test p values are planned.

Predictor baseline: original questionnaire categories for age, education, locality, marital status and employment. Retain exact released categories (trim surrounding whitespace), excluding refused/don't-know/not-applicable and missing values. In particular, do not equate the UN Women work activities with DHS cash/kind categories, or collapse the five UN Women age bands into the original DHS bands. Keep generic `Married` separate from the more specific spouse-residence categories. These are predictors in the new survey, not harmonized individual DHS records. No religion field is available in the released inventory. No country indicators or direct-IPV variable will be included: country indicators cannot predict new countries and direct physical-IPV exposure is asked only in Colombia.

Primary added feature: historical national female acceptance. In the secondary six-country set, compare the same individual baseline with each of national acceptance, education higher-minus-none (pp), and rural-minus-urban (pp), one at a time. Education sign matches the original contrast table and is opposite to the earlier GFS no-education-minus-higher feature. No imputation of incomplete country profiles. Retain differing source years and population universes explicitly; historical population estimates are contextual descriptors, not each woman's attitudes or experiences. The UN Women adult population is not the same as the historical DHS population.

Model: fixed ridge alpha=10, matching the earlier GFS comparison; no hyperparameter search or neural embeddings. Full one-hot encoding of categorical predictors, training-only category fitting, unknown held-out categories encoded as all zeros with an exported count ledger. Standardize the added numerical context feature on training rows only (unweighted preprocessing); categorical features are not standardized. Normalize supplied respondent `weight` within each training country, then rescale to total training N so countries have equal total mass. Train and score using those weights. This fixes the penalty scale and prevents countries with larger samples dominating. Hold out each country in turn; no individual random split. Known independent historical covariates of a target country may enter prediction; its symptom outcomes may not enter fitting, scaling or selection.

Save a training weighted-mean reference and the individual-only and context models. Primary metric: supplied-weight MAE within each held-out country, averaged equally over countries. Also save weighted RMSE and R2, including negative values, as secondary diagnostics. Prediction clipping is not planned: report any predictions outside theoretical score bounds. Save paired context-minus-individual country MAE and counts improving. Negative change means improvement. Do not interpret a small or inconsistent average reduction as clinically useful; compare with the training-mean reference. No confidence interval or p value across overlapping folds will be manufactured; these selected six/eight countries do not support precise general population claims.

QC: verify source hashes, country/ID uniqueness, positive weights, coding and cohort ledgers, complete five-item contrasts, disjoint country/individual splits, training weight balance, and all expected folds. Independently reproduce ridge predictions by solving the penalized weighted normal equations, reconstruct country metrics and mean-reference errors, and check each context increment. Save no respondent rows or predictions in review outputs. Save software versions, aggregate tables, a vector PDF/SVG and 600-dpi figure. Retain every comparison regardless of direction. No main-manuscript rewrite during this comparison.
