# A fixed graph encoder with conditional Bayesian country–scenario readout

Prepared 22 October 2026 before these new fits. Previously examined outcomes and earlier competing-model results make this retrospective exploratory validation. This is one fixed hybrid architecture, not a new GNN algorithm or an architecture search.

## Question and separation of populations

Does learning a nonlinear graph representation improve the completed national mean-versus-centered-scenario hierarchy, and does the alignment of predictor-defined neighbours contribute beyond self-only or random messages? The ENDES Ising model is a separate individual-response study. Its conditional response edges do not identify national country edges and are not used here.

Reuse the completed Bayesian study's current questionnaire-compatible 42 strict country/time and 19 external targets, retaining every eligible donor round. All target-country attitude history is absent; all donor fieldwork ends precede target fieldwork under the existing external month-end convention. Nepal 2011 is excluded. These 61 evaluations are not 61 new independent populations. No new donor sensitivity, prior search, feature or outcome is introduced. This study does not observe violence incidence, mental health or brain function.

## Training-only preparation and graph

Copy public aggregate inputs and bindings from the completed Bayesian hierarchy. For each target, verify exact source-corrected membership, fieldwork rules and outcome values. Impute and standardize the five features (calendar, HDI, electoral democracy, liberal democracy, women's political empowerment) using donor rounds only, with preprocessing weight one divided by the number of that country's eligible rounds. Target values never affect fitting, graph construction, scaling or representations.

A node is a country–survey round, not a new independent country. Construct directed five-nearest-neighbour training graphs using Euclidean distance in the four standardized structural features; exclude every same-country round. Deterministic ties follow original donor order. Use Gaussian weights with one bandwidth equal to the median positive selected training-neighbour distance (one if all distances are zero), then row normalize. The query receives five nearest training rounds and the same bandwidth, without feedback to training nodes. All inputs to message passing are predictors; training outcomes never appear as node features.

Three arms use identical parameter shapes and starting arrays: real neighbours; self-only (all neighbour messages zero); and random cross-country donor neighbours, drawn without replacement with a fixed target-specific seed. Random controls retain the sorted real neighbour weights, replacing only donor identities. Query random neighbours are drawn only from permitted training rounds. The self-only arm has inactive neighbour parameters; equal arrays do not imply equal functional capacity. Repeated country effects are modelled in the readout, not as independent country observations.

## Fixed neural encoder

For standardized predictor row z_i, let h_i = tanh(z_i W_self + sum_j A_ij z_j W_neighbour + b), with four units. A separate linear training head predicts standardized outcomes from [z_i, h_i, 1]. One encoder predicts the arithmetic mean of the five percentages; a separate encoder jointly predicts four fixed Helmert contrast coordinates. This separation ensures G1/G2 use exactly the same learned mean, so the centered-profile increment does not change the mean encoder.

For each branch, standardize the training outcomes using the same one-total-per-country weights. Fit country-weighted mean squared error plus 0.01 times the mean squared value of all parameters, including biases. There are no random effects in this representation-learning objective; the final hierarchical readout is fitted afterward. Use 400 full-batch Adam steps, learning rate 0.01, float64 CPU, fixed seeds 42 and 43, Xavier-uniform matrices, zero biases. No early stopping, inner selection or outer-outcome selection. Seed 42 is primary; seed 43 is a separately retained optimization sensitivity, not a chain or posterior mixture. Save all final arrays, objectives and training diagnostics. Nonfinite fits fail explicitly.

There are 61 targets × three graph arms × two branches × two seeds = 732 encoder fits, with at most two one-thread CPU workers. A synthetic timing and gradient/forward check precedes freezing; it cannot inspect target outcomes or change hyperparameters based on prediction performance.

## Exact Bayesian hierarchical readout conditional on the encoder

After training, discard the auxiliary neural training head for forecasting. Standardize each learned hidden feature using training country weights only (constant features receive scale one). Fit the final design [intercept, five original standardized predictors, four learned standardized hidden features]. A linear-only design [intercept, five predictors] is reconstructed as a numerical/source check against the completed B2 hierarchy.

For each coordinate independently, y = X beta + C u + epsilon, epsilon|sigma² ~ N(0,sigma²I), beta|sigma² ~ N(m0,sigma²V0), u|sigma²,lambda ~ N(0,lambda sigma² I), sigma² ~ IG(2,100), with uniform prior over lambda in {0,0.25,1,4,16}. Intercept conditional variance is 25, every standardized slope variance is 1; mean intercept prior is 25 percentage points, contrast intercept priors zero. Integrate coefficients, donor-country effects, residual variance and the finite lambda mixture by the completed normal–inverse-gamma algebra. Every donor round enters once in the Gaussian likelihood. Do not invent binomial counts or sampling standard errors.

For a new country, add lambda sigma² to predictive variance and do not estimate its random effect from held-out outcomes. G1 combines the learned mean-branch readout with unchanged linear B2 profile coordinates. G2 combines exactly the same learned mean with the independently trained profile-branch readout. The frozen full linear B2 baseline remains separately visible. Raw G1/G2 means must match exactly; G1 raw profiles must match B2 exactly. Any bounded differences caused by clipping are distinguished from learned effects.

This is a Bayesian-readout GNN. Neural weights are point estimates learned using outcomes, and readout inference conditions on those learned features. Consequently its intervals omit representation learning, architecture selection and full retraining uncertainty; they are not a full Bayesian posterior over the entire pipeline, calibrated national design intervals or causal uncertainty. The extra learned features also alter the conditional prior geometry. Graph ablations, not a claim of equal effective prior capacity, assess their contribution.

## Evaluation, intervals and preservation

Save exact posterior mean predictions and raw/bounded (0–100) component, mean, centered-profile and pair-ordering losses. Verify MSE = squared mean error + centered-profile MSE for every prediction. Observed scenario ties are excluded; predicted gaps ≤1e-10 percentage points receive half credit. The main contrasts are real G2 versus linear B2, real versus self-only G2, real versus random G2, and real G2 versus G1 centered profile. Retain all arms and both seeds regardless of direction. Fixed paired equal-country resampling (10,000 draws) describes saved-error uncertainty, not new training, national survey variance or independent-training-set uncertainty.

Use fixed 20,000 predictive draws to reconstruct five-scenario 50/80/95% intervals and out-of-range probabilities, explicitly conditional on the learned encoder. Compare independent half-stream 95% endpoints in the five scenarios and five coordinates; if their maximum discrepancy exceeds 2 pp, use the fixed 100,000 stream and disclose any remaining failure. Point means are analytic and unaffected. Keep Gaussian out-of-range mass, raw and bounded losses visible; do not imply clinical or prospective calibration.
