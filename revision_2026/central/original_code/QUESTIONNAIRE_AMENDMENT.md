# Questionnaire-compatibility amendment

Saved 20 October 2026, **after the original three fixed cases had fitted and before fitting the additional questionnaire-compatible case**. The original protocol and all three planned cases remain intact. This amendment is motivated by a parallel source-document audit, not by comparative prediction performance or a searched exclusion set.

The source audit identified an explicit limitation in the Nepal DHS 2011 final report, FR257 printed page 228, footnote 1. A general wife-beating filter question (Q824A) restricted administration of the five scenario questions to a very small subgroup; the report states that the scenario data cannot meaningfully be used. The standardized API percentages were previously retained as flagged anomalies. They are now documented as questionnaire-incompatible for this specific five-component target. The Benin 2012 report, in contrast, confirms the five direct scenario prompts and published values, so Benin is retained in this amendment's case. The final source ledger and hashes are maintained in the parallel measurement-comparability audit and referenced in the generated report.

Add exactly one fourth, separately labelled case, `questionnaire_compatible`: exclude `NP2011DHS` from donor rounds; retain `BJ2012DHS`; otherwise use exactly the original primary dates, target-country exclusion, weights, imputation, standardization, alpha, models, 42 latest targets, and bounded/raw evaluation. Nepal's latest target is not 2011, so target counts do not change. Do not modify the original `primary`, `anomaly_removed`, or `common_2020` definitions or select between cases by results.

The new case will be appended after the three original cases when generating deterministic country bootstrap draws, leaving their resampling sequence unchanged. Its source-based exclusion improves this known measurement issue but does not establish full cross-survey construct equivalence. All outcomes remain previously inspected and all comparisons remain exploratory.
