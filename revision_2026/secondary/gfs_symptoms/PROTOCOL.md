# Public GFS extension: protocol fixed before outcome modelling

Date: 2026-10-19. This is an exploratory addition, not a preregistration. Dataset availability and questionnaire coding were inspected before this document; outcome models had not been fitted.

The public wave-2 wide release includes baseline and follow-up responses. Use baseline women aged 18–99 (99 means 99+) in the nine countries overlapping the legacy country inventory. Report the observed interview dates, actual release sample counts and exclusions rather than importing counts from an earlier publication. No respondent is linked to DHS respondents.

Four symptom questions refer to the preceding two weeks. Original codes 1/2/3/4 mean nearly every day / more than half the days / several days / not at all. Recode each as 4 minus the original valid value. Sum depressed mood and lack of interest into PHQ-2 (0–6), anxiety and uncontrollable worry into GAD-2 (0–6), and all four into PHQ-4 (0–12). Use complete items only. PHQ-2 and GAD-2 >=3 indicate positive screens, not diagnosed disorders. COVID exposure is reported death of a family member or close friend, not personal infection or a dated event. Baseline is after the acute 2020 pandemic; no causal pandemic comparison is possible.

1. Describe women's screening outcomes by country using released cross-sectional weights, strata and PSU. Construct designs on the full valid adult sample, then use women as a subpopulation. Country-specific weight scales do not imply population-size weights when countries are pooled. For pooled associations, normalize weights within each country to equal total country mass. Use Taylor-linearized first-stage design uncertainty; no finite-population correction is supplied. Record singleton strata and use the survey package's adjustment where necessary.
2. Fit two separate survey-weighted logistic models for PHQ-2 and GAD-2 positive screens against COVID bereavement. Both include country fixed effects, age per decade and its square, education (three original levels), employment (original levels), marital status (original levels), income difficulty, urbanicity (four original levels) and religious-service attendance (five original levels). Analyze complete cases, report exclusions. Report odds ratios, confidence intervals and Holm-adjusted p values across the two primary COVID coefficients. Sensitivities: omit attendance; restrict age 18–49; report per-country crude weighted associations. Results are associations subject to unmeasured confounding, selection and recall.
3. Separately test prediction of continuous PHQ-4 in leave-one-country-out folds. Compare weighted training-mean, ridge, histogram gradient boosting, PCA (three dimensions) plus ridge, supervised CEBRA (three dimensions) plus ridge and a shuffled-auxiliary-label CEBRA control. Compare demographic/economic/religious/bereavement predictors with the same predictors plus a single official historical national female wife-beating-acceptance percentage (any standardized reason). Preserve survey year, population coverage and wording limitations. This contextual percentage is not an individual attitude or contemporaneous exposure.
4. Fixed predictive settings: seeds 42/43/44 for CEBRA; 1000 training iterations, offset1 architecture, 32 hidden units, batch 128, cosine distance, delta conditional with delta .3, learning rate .001; standardized training PHQ-4 as continuous auxiliary label. Ridge alpha 10, HGB 100 iterations/7 leaves/minimum leaf 30/l2 10, PCA three dimensions. Fit transformations only on training countries. To bound compute, select at most 1000 women per training country using seed 2026; all models use identical selected women. Fit/score weighted baselines and readouts with within-country normalized weights. CEBRA representation sampling itself is unweighted; this limitation must be reported. Test all eligible women in the held-out country. Average repeated CEBRA seeds within country, then average countries equally; show individual countries, seed variability and the context-minus-baseline difference. No test-label tuning or claim of confirmatory improvement.
5. An optional longitudinal sensitivity is secondary and only feasible with baseline/follow-up complete items and valid longitudinal weights. It must not replace the pre-specified baseline outcomes silently.

The country context sample is nine units, regardless of the number of women. Do not infer individual normative effects, IPV prevalence, clinical diagnoses or a causal effect of COVID. This extension cannot replace the original DHS respondent analyses requested in the rejection; those remain unavailable without authorized files.
